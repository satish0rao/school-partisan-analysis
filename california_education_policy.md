# California's Education Policy versus Evidence

## My question to Claude:

> *"It seems rigor is pretty settled and reconfirmed in the CA data. What are the legislators / state board of education doing?"*

## Background.

> This question is in a long analysis I did with Claude. It follows up and corrects years-old analysis of mine, which suggested that for at least some African American students, liberals are bad — maybe very bad. It is long and involved. You can find a summary of findings, the code, and (for transparency) the <mark>[entire conversation transcript](https://github.com/satish0rao/school-partisan-analysis/blob/master/conversation_transcript.md)</mark> on the <mark>[GitHub repository](https://github.com/satish0rao/school-partisan-analysis)</mark>.


## The answer

Mostly moving in the *opposite* direction, despite the evidence. The California Department of Education and Legislature have spent the last 5–10 years implementing essentially the package the data suggests doesn't work, and resisting the package that does.

### What CA has been doing (mostly the wrong direction)

**1. 2023 Math Framework.** Adopted after years of contentious revisions. De-emphasizes 8th-grade algebra, frames math through "equity" and "social justice" lenses, encourages "data science" tracks as substitutes for the calculus pipeline. The State Board approved it over substantial pushback from Stanford, Harvard, and 1,200+ STEM faculty signatories. Implementation is now rolling out. SFUSD's 2014 algebra ban was the proof-of-concept that this framework essentially codifies.

**2. Anti-tracking / "de-leveling".** Multiple liberal-affluent districts (Sequoia Union, Culver City, Mountain View-Los Altos, San Mateo Union, among others) have eliminated honors courses or de-tracked entirely. Parental pushback is intense. Equity messaging dominates the rationale. Achievement-gap effects so far: not improving.

**3. Ethnic Studies (AB 101, 2021).** Mandates ethnic studies as a HS graduation requirement statewide starting 2025–26. The "Liberated Ethnic Studies Model Curriculum" — opposed by Jewish groups, ADL, others for ideological framing — is in use in some districts as an alternative to the state-revised model curriculum.

**4. Charter restrictions (AB 1505, 2019).** Made it easier for districts to deny charter applications. New charter approvals dropped significantly. CTA (CA Teachers Association) lobbied for this; the high-rigor charter networks (KIPP, Aspire, Rocketship) have struggled to expand.

**5. Reading instruction — slow and reluctant.** California has not adopted Mississippi-style structured-literacy mandates. Whole language and balanced literacy still dominant. Recent CA legislation has begun requiring *some* structured-literacy elements after years of advocacy, but implementation is voluntary and slow. CA still doesn't require evidence-based reading curricula at the district level.

**6. Weakened accountability framework.** Various tweaks have made standardized testing less central to school evaluation. Score-based accountability has been weakened over the past decade.

**7. CDE Equity Office, Anti-Bias initiatives, Cultural Responsiveness frameworks.** Most major State Board investments have been in the equity-pedagogy direction. Much less institutional energy has gone into Mississippi-style accountability + rigor + evidence-based instruction.

### The counter-currents (small but real)

- **Voter pushback.** SF Prop G (2024) reversed SFUSD's 2014 algebra ban with 82% support. Three SF school board members (Collins, López, Moliga) were recalled in 2022 over progressive-overreach concerns. Suggests parent/voter sentiment is moving in the opposite direction from the institutional establishment.
- **Tony Thurmond** (State Superintendent) has occasionally voiced support for reading reform, but the institutional inertia is large.
- **Some legislators** (Marc Berman, Rebecca Bauer-Kahan) have pushed for accountability and reading reform — these efforts are minority positions.
- **Parent advocacy groups** like Decoding Dyslexia CA have pushed for evidence-based reading instruction. Generally treated as outsider voices by the institution.

### What's NOT being seriously discussed at the state level

- Mississippi-style structured literacy mandate
- Florida-style A–F school accountability
- Expanding rigorous charter networks like KIPP
- Restoring rigorous tracking and AP pipelines
- Teacher accountability tied to student outcomes
- Black-achievement-specific intervention programs at scale
- Evidence-based curriculum mandates

### The political dynamics

CTA (teachers' union) is one of the most powerful interest groups in CA Democratic politics. They have generally opposed charter expansion, test-score-based accountability measures, and top-down curriculum mandates. The Democratic supermajority in Sacramento is closely aligned with CTA. The State Board is appointed by the Governor (Newsom) and reflects that alignment.

So the answer to "what are they doing" is roughly: doubling down on the equity-pedagogy / anti-rigor / anti-accountability package, while voters and parents (especially in the most progressive districts) increasingly push back. The institutional momentum is in the direction the data suggests is producing the bad outcomes.

The recent Black-parent activism around literacy in Oakland and Sacramento, the SF algebra-ban reversal, and the school-board recalls are early indicators that political coalitions may be shifting. But the legislative and State Board direction remains essentially counter to the empirical evidence.

It's a textbook case of an interest-group equilibrium producing policies that don't serve the constituency the rhetoric claims to serve. The Black students whose outcomes were measured aren't well-served by the current direction; the institution serving them isn't actually responsive to them.

## Background context

This response came at the end of a long analytical session examining California school test scores (2012–2025) joined to 2016 precinct-level vote share. The analysis established (caveats and methodological notes in the <mark>[main repo](https://github.com/satish0rao/school-partisan-analysis)</mark>):

- Liberal California precincts have larger Black-white achievement gaps in their schools than conservative precincts
- The pattern survives demographic controls and is concentrated in the non-economically-disadvantaged subgroup at the high-school level
- The mechanism is two-sided: white middle-class students score *higher* in liberal precincts; Black middle-class HS students score *lower*
- Cross-state comparisons (Mississippi, Florida, Texas outperforming CA on Black-student NAEP despite worse demographics) corroborate that policy/political-coalition layer matters independent of demographics
- The pattern is consistent with the "soft bigotry of low expectations" framework articulated by figures across the political spectrum (Bush, Obama, Loury, McWhorter, Sowell)

For full data, methodology, code, and the complete back-and-forth conversation that produced these conclusions — including where the user challenged framings and where corrections were made — see the <mark>[GitHub repo](https://github.com/satish0rao/school-partisan-analysis)</mark>.

## A note on teachers and the CTA

I'm pro-teacher. I genuinely revere classroom teachers — the work they do is hard, important, and substantially undervalued. They should be paid more.

The California Teachers Association largely argues for teacher pay, benefits, and working conditions, which I applaud. The CTA also takes positions on pedagogical and policy questions, and on those questions reasonable people — including teachers themselves — sometimes hold different views from the institutional one. Teachers want their kids to learn. They want clear standards. They want to be supported in that. 

When the empirical evidence on what produces better student outcomes points in one direction and a particular policy position points another, the right move is to follow the evidence. Being pro-teacher, paying them more, and being honest about which pedagogical approaches work are not in tension.
